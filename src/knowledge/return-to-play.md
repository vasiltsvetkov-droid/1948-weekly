## Key Postulates

1. **RTP must be criteria-based, not time-based.** Time alone is an insufficient predictor of readiness. Criteria-based RTP using LSI, hop tests, strength tests, GPS benchmarks, and psychological readiness produces significantly lower reinjury rates (Grindem et al. 2016).

2. **LSI >= 90% is the universal RTP threshold** for all hop tests (single hop, triple hop, cross-over hop, 6-m timed hop) and isokinetic strength (quad and hamstring at 60 deg/s and 240 deg/s). ACL patients passing all 6 RTP criteria had 10% reinjury rate vs 38% in those who failed (Grindem et al. 2016).

3. **Psychological readiness is a mandatory RTP criterion.** ACL-RSI >= 65 is required for clearance. Players scoring <40 have significantly higher reinjury rates. Do not clear for contact practice if ACL-RSI <65 regardless of physical criteria (Webster et al. 2018).

4. **GPS-based graded exposure provides objective load progression.** RTP should follow a structured GPS progression from 40-60% of position-average match load through to >95% before full match clearance.

5. **Match exposure must be progressively loaded.** First match back: 20-30 min (substitute). Gradual increase: 30-45 min over 2-3 matches. Full match exposure: 45-60 min over 3-5 matches. Unrestricted 90 min only after full GPS benchmarking.

6. **Re-injury risk remains 2-fold elevated in the first 2 weeks post-RTP** with exponential decline toward baseline over approximately 4 weeks (Gabbett et al. 2017). Load should not exceed 85% of normal match/training load in the first week back.

7. **Each month RTP is delayed (up to 9 months post-ACL) reduces reinjury risk by 51%.** Do not clear for RTP before 9 months regardless of criteria satisfaction, particularly for players under age 25 (Grindem et al. 2016).

## Thresholds & Decision Rules

### Limb Symmetry Index (LSI) Criteria

| Test | Minimum RTP Threshold |
|------|----------------------|
| Single Hop for Distance | >= 90% |
| Triple Hop for Distance | >= 90% |
| Cross-over Hop | >= 90% |
| 6-meter Timed Hop | >= 90% |
| Isokinetic Quad Strength (60 deg/s) | >= 90% |
| Isokinetic Hamstring Strength (60 deg/s) | >= 90% |
| CMJ Symmetry | >= 90% |

### ACL 6-Criterion RTP Model

| Criterion | Threshold | Evidence |
|-----------|-----------|----------|
| ROM | Full (0-135 deg minimum) | Clinical standard |
| Effusion | Trace or absent | Clinical standard |
| Quadriceps Strength LSI | >= 90% | Grindem et al. 2016 |
| Hamstring Strength LSI | >= 90% | Grindem et al. 2016 |
| Hop Test Battery LSI | >= 90% | Grindem et al. 2016 |
| Psychological Readiness (ACL-RSI) | >= 65 points | Webster et al. 2018 |

### Psychological Readiness Thresholds

| Measure | Threshold | Interpretation |
|---------|-----------|---------------|
| ACL-RSI | >= 65 | Cleared for RTP |
| ACL-RSI | 40-64 | Caution zone; address kinesiophobia |
| ACL-RSI | <40 | High reinjury risk; do not clear |
| Tampa Scale (TSK-17) | >37 | High kinesiophobia; elevated reinjury risk |
| Player confidence | >= 7/10 | Minimum self-reported readiness |

### GPS Graded Exposure Targets (% of Position-Average Match Load)

| Phase | TD | HSR | Sprint Distance | Accelerations |
|-------|----|-----|-----------------|--------------|
| Training reintegration (individual) | 40-60% | 20-40% | 10-20% | 40-60% |
| Group training (modified) | 60-75% | 40-60% | 20-40% | 60-75% |
| Full team training | 75-90% | 60-80% | 40-60% | 75-90% |
| Match readiness | >90% | >80% | >70% | >85% |
| Full match clearance | >95% | >90% | >85% | >90% |

### Match Exposure Progression

| Stage | Minutes | Intensity | Notes |
|-------|---------|-----------|-------|
| First match back (partial) | 20-30 min | Substitute, managed role | Avoid extra time |
| Gradual increase | 30-45 min | Partial match | 2-3 matches |
| Full match exposure | 45-60 min | First half or structured sub | 3-5 matches |
| Unrestricted | 90 min | Full availability | After full GPS benchmarking |

### ACL Time-Based Reinjury Risk

| RTP Timing | Reinjury Risk |
|------------|---------------|
| 6 months | ~34% |
| 9 months | ~14% |
| 12 months | ~8% |
| Each month delay (up to 9 mo) | -51% risk per month |

### Hamstring Strain RTP Milestones

| Phase | Timeline | Key Criteria to Progress |
|-------|----------|------------------------|
| Phase 1: Acute | Days 1-5 | Pain-free walking; no passive SLR pain at 70 deg |
| Phase 2: Subacute | Days 5-14 / up to 4 wk | Pain-free Nordic at full extension; >= 70% isokinetic LSI |
| Phase 3: Return to Training | 2-6 weeks | Pain-free at 100% max speed; LSI >= 90% |
| Phase 4: Return to Play | Criteria-based | Full team training x3 sessions; LSI >= 90%; confidence >= 7/10 |

### Re-Injury Risk Window Post-RTP

| Period | Risk Level | Load Recommendation |
|--------|-----------|-------------------|
| Week 1 post-RTP | 2x elevated | Max 85% of normal match/training load |
| Week 2 post-RTP | ~1.5x elevated | Progressive increase; daily CMJ and morning HRV |
| Weeks 3-4 post-RTP | Declining toward baseline | Normal loading with close monitoring |
| Week 5+ post-RTP | Near baseline | Standard monitoring |

## Analysis Templates

### General RTP Context

```
RETURN TO PLAY FRAMEWORK:
This knowledge base provides general RTP criteria and GPS benchmarking frameworks for
clinical decision-making. RTP data is not directly triggered by current weekly GPS metrics
but serves as a reference when players are returning from injury.

KEY PRINCIPLES:
1. All RTP decisions must satisfy the LSI >= 90% criterion across hop tests and strength.
2. Psychological clearance (ACL-RSI >= 65) is mandatory before contact training.
3. GPS graded exposure should progress through 5 phases: 40-60% -> 60-75% -> 75-90% -> >90% -> >95%.
4. Match exposure should build from 20-30 min substitute appearances to unrestricted 90 min.
5. Re-injury risk is 2x elevated in the first 2 weeks post-RTP; load capping at 85% in week 1.
6. Monitor CMJ daily and HRV each morning during the first 2 weeks of RTP.

INJURY-SPECIFIC PROTOCOLS:
- Hamstring: Askling L-protocol (Extender, Diver, Glider); eccentric-biased rehab
- ACL: 9-month minimum; 6-criterion model; phase-specific progression
- Ankle: Balance board training reduces re-sprain by 68% (Tropp et al. 1985)
- Quadriceps: Kicking assessment required before clearance
- Adductor: Copenhagen Adductor integration as early as pain-free isometric
- Bone stress: MRI-graded; high-risk locations (femoral neck, navicular, Jones) require extended timelines
```

## Chart Specifications

None. RTP data is not currently captured in the weekly GPS monitoring system. RTP tracking requires clinical data (LSI scores, hop tests, psychological inventories) that exist outside the current data pipeline.
