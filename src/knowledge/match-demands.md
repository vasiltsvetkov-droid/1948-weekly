## Key Postulates

1. **Match physical demands define the reference standard for all training load prescription.** Total distance (10,000-13,000 m), HSR >5.5 m/s (900-1,200 m), sprint distance >7.0 m/s (200-400 m), and accelerations >3 m/s^2 (120-180) represent the elite male match profile and serve as the denominator for all weekly load-percentage targets (Bangsbo 1994; Bradley et al. 2009; Di Salvo et al. 2009).

2. **Metabolic power analysis reveals that speed-based thresholds underestimate true high-intensity activity.** 26% of match distance accounts for 42% of total energy expenditure. The metabolic power approach captures ~35% more high-intensity activity than speed-based thresholds alone, because high-intensity efforts (accelerations, decelerations) frequently occur at low absolute speeds (Osgnach et al. 2010).

3. **Energy expenditure during a match is approximately 65 kJ/kg (~1,600 kcal for a 75 kg player).** This establishes the nutritional recovery requirements and informs the total NRG metric used in the monitoring system.

4. **The aerobic system provides 80-90% of total match energy.** Despite the high-intensity appearance of football, the predominant energy system is aerobic, with average HR at 85-90% HRmax and VO2max utilization at 70-80% of maximum during match play (Bangsbo 1994; Stolen et al. 2005).

5. **Congested schedules (>= 2 matches/week) require 25-40% training volume reduction.** In congested periods, high-intensity SSGs should be replaced with tactical walkthroughs, strength sessions removed (maintain mobility only), and squad rotation of 3-5 players per match implemented.

6. **Less than 72 h between matches produces significant performance decrements.** Sprint distance decreases 5-15%, HSR decreases 10-20%, and jump height decreases 5-10% when inter-match recovery is insufficient.

## Thresholds & Decision Rules

### Match Physical Profile (Elite Male)

| Metric | Mean Value | Range | Source |
|--------|-----------|-------|--------|
| Total Distance | 10,000-13,000 m | 9,500-14,000 m | Bangsbo 1994; Bradley et al. 2009 |
| HSR (>5.5 m/s) | 900-1,200 m | 700-1,500 m | Di Salvo et al. 2009 |
| Sprint Distance (>7.0 m/s) | 200-400 m | 150-600 m | Di Salvo et al. 2007 |
| Number of Sprints | 30-60 | 20-100 | Bradley et al. 2009 |
| Accelerations (>3 m/s^2) | 120-180 | 80-250 | Osgnach et al. 2010 |
| Decelerations (>3 m/s^2) | 100-160 | 70-220 | Dalen et al. 2016 |
| High-Intensity Actions | 1,500-2,500 | - | Bangsbo et al. 2006 |

### Metabolic & Energy System Profile

| Parameter | Value | Source |
|-----------|-------|--------|
| Energy expenditure | ~65 kJ/kg per match (~1,600 kcal for 75 kg) | Osgnach et al. 2010 |
| Aerobic contribution | 80-90% of total energy | Bangsbo 1994 |
| Anaerobic alactic (PCr) | 6-8% total energy | Critical for sprints <6 s |
| Anaerobic lactic (glycolysis) | 5-10% | Active in repeated sprint sequences |
| VO2max utilization | 70-80% of maximum | Stolen et al. 2005 |
| Average match HR | 85-90% HRmax | Bangsbo 1994 |

### Congested Schedule Thresholds

| Parameter | Threshold | Action |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| Congested definition | >= 2 matches/week or 3+ in 7 days | Activate congested protocols |
| Training volume reduction | 25-40% | Reduce session duration to 60-75% of normal |
| HSR reduction | 30-50% | Replace high-intensity SSGs with tactical walkthroughs |
| Strength sessions | Remove | Maintain mobility only during congested periods |
| Squad rotation | 3-5 players per match | Individual load management |
| Minimum inter-match recovery | 72 h preferred | <72 h = significant performance decrements |

### Congested Schedule Performance Decrements (<72 h Recovery)

| Metric | Performance Decline |
|--------|-------------------|
| Sprint distance | -5 to -15% |
| HSR distance | -10 to -20% |
| Jump height (CMJ) | -5 to -10% |

### Two-Match Week Microcycle

| Day | Training Content | Load (% match) |
|-----|----------------|----------------|
| MD (Match 1) | Competition | 100% |
| MD+1 | Pool recovery, mobility | 15-20% |
| MD+2 / MD-2 (pre-match 2) | Light technical, tactical walkthrough | 20-30% |
| MD-1 (pre-match 2) | Activation only, set pieces | 15-25% |
| MD (Match 2) | Competition | 100% |

### Recovery Priority Order (Congested Periods)

| Priority | Intervention | Detail |
|----------|-------------|--------|
| 1 | Sleep | Target 9-10 h; most impactful recovery modality |
| 2 | Nutrition | CHO 1-1.5 g/kg within 30 min post-match |
| 3 | Hydration | Replace 150% of fluid lost |
| 4 | Contrast therapy | Cold water immersion or contrast baths |
| 5 | Active recovery | Pool walking, light bike (MD+1 only) |

## Analysis Templates

### Match Demand Context

```
MATCH DEMAND REFERENCE FRAMEWORK:
The elite male football match demands serve as the reference standard for all weekly
load-percentage calculations in the monitoring system.

REFERENCE VALUES:
- Total Distance: 10,000-13,000 m per match
- HSR (>5.5 m/s): 900-1,200 m per match
- Sprint Distance (>7.0 m/s): 200-400 m per match
- Accelerations (>3 m/s^2): 120-180 per match
- Energy Expenditure: ~65 kJ/kg per match (~1,600 kcal for 75 kg player)

WEEKLY TRAINING vs MATCH LOAD:
- Weekly HSR training dose should target 0.6-0.9x of match HSR for lowest injury risk
  (Malone et al. 2017)
- Volumes below 0.5x match load: elevated injury risk (under-preparation)
- Volumes above 1.2x match load: elevated injury risk (overload)

CONGESTED SCHEDULE ALERT:
When >= 2 matches occur in a single week, training volume should be reduced by 25-40%.
High-intensity SSGs should be replaced with tactical walkthroughs, and strength sessions
removed (maintain mobility only). Squad rotation of 3-5 players per match is recommended
for individual load management.
```

## Chart Specifications

None. Match demand data serves as reference values for weekly load-percentage calculations. Match-level GPS data is embedded in the system's match reference values rather than charted independently.
