# Periodization

Knowledge domain covering MD-based microcycle structure, mesocycle planning, and periodization models for elite football.

**Source sections:** §8 (Weekly Microcycle Planning), §10 (Monthly Mesocycle Planning), §11 (Periodization Models)

---

## Key Postulates

1. **The MD-based microcycle** organizes all training days relative to Match Day (MD). The highest training intensity day is MD-4; load tapers progressively toward MD-1. MD+1 and MD+2 are dedicated to recovery (Malone et al. 2015; Buchheit et al. 2013).

2. **Rest on MD+2 is associated with 2–3× lower non-contact injury rates** in elite football (Dupont et al. 2010). Starters from the previous match should have mandatory off or pool-only on MD+2.

3. **Speed has the shortest residual training effect (5±3 days)** of all physical qualities (Issurin 2008). Maximal speed work must appear at least once per microcycle, ideally at MD-4, to prevent detraining.

4. **The 4-week mesocycle block** follows a Loading → Overloading → Recovery/Deload → Competition Peak structure. The deload week (Week 3) reduces volume by 30–40% while maintaining moderate intensity to produce supercompensation.

5. **Tactical periodization (Frade/Mourinho)** treats physical, tactical, technical, and psychological dimensions as inseparable. All conditioning emerges from game-model-specific exercises. Horizontal alternation ensures no dominant energy system is trained on consecutive days.

6. **Block periodization (Issurin 2008)** organizes training in concentrated blocks of Accumulation (3–4 weeks, volume-based), Transmutation (3–4 weeks, sport-specific conversion), and Realization (1–2 weeks, peaking). Evidence supports block periodization as never inferior to traditional approaches.

7. **Daily Undulating Periodization (DUP)** alternates loading schemes within the week (e.g., heavy, moderate, power days). DUP is superior to linear periodization for strength gains over 12 weeks (Rhea et al. 2002) and suits in-season maintenance where training days vary in character.

## Thresholds & Decision Rules

### MD-Based HSR Distribution Targets

| Day | % of Match HSR | Session Type | Rationale |
|-----|---------------|-------------|-----------|
| MD | 100% | Match | Competition reference |
| MD+1 | 10–15% | Active Recovery | Recovery flush; no sprinting; no eccentric loading |
| MD+2 | 0–10% or rest | Rest / Minimal | Minimal eccentric load; rest preferred |
| MD-4 | 70–80% | High Intensity Re-activation | Highest intensity training day; sprint work, SSGs |
| MD-3 | 55–65% | Acquisition / Tactical Development | Moderate; highest total volume day |
| MD-2 | 40–55% | Match Preparation | Load reduction begins; avoid sprint >95% Vmax |
| MD-1 | 25–35% | Activation / Prime | Minimal; speed activations only |

### Weekly HSR Training Dose

| Metric | Target | Source |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Total weekly HSR vs match HSR | 0.6–0.9× | Malone et al. 2017 |
| MD-4 sprints above 90% Vmax | 8–12 sprints | Buchheit et al. 2013 |
| MD-1 maximal sprints | 3–5 sprints | Activation protocol |

### 4-Week Mesocycle Block Structure

| Week | Phase | Volume | Intensity | Focus |
|------|-------|--------|-----------|-------|
| Week 1 | Loading | High | Moderate | Accumulation; fitness base |
| Week 2 | Overloading | High | High | Intensification; peak stimulus |
| Week 3 | Recovery / Deload | Low (−30–40%) | Moderate | Supercompensation; restoration |
| Week 4 | Competition Peak | Moderate | High | Performance realization |

### Residual Training Effects (Maintenance Frequencies)

| Quality | Residual Duration | Minimum Maintenance Frequency |
|---------|-------------------|-------------------------------|
| Speed (max velocity) | 5±3 days | 3–4 sessions/week (every microcycle) |
| Power / Speed-strength | 15±5 days | 2 sessions/week |
| Anaerobic capacity | 18±4 days | 2 sessions/week |
| Strength endurance | 15–25 days | 2 sessions/week |
| Maximal strength | 30±5 days | 1 session/week |
| Aerobic endurance | 25–35 days | 1–2 sessions/week |

## Analysis Templates

### Microcycle Quality Assessment

```
MICROCYCLE STRUCTURE EVALUATION:

1. HSR Distribution Check:
   - MD+1 HSR should be 10–15% of match HSR
   - MD-4 HSR should be 70–80% of match HSR (highest training day)
   - MD-1 HSR should be 25–35% of match HSR
   - Weekly total HSR should be 0.6–0.9× match HSR

2. Volume Tapering Check:
   - MD-4 should have highest session load
   - Each subsequent day (MD-3 → MD-2 → MD-1) should show progressive load reduction
   - MD+1 and MD+2 should have lowest load values

3. Speed Exposure Check:
   - At least one session must include sprints at ≥90% Vmax (ideally MD-4)
   - Speed residual effect is only 5±3 days — if no maximal speed work
    in the microcycle, neuromuscular detraining is occurring

4. Monotony Check:
   - Training monotony (mean daily load / SD) should be < 2.0
   - Values > 2.0 indicate insufficient day-to-day variation
    (Foster et al. 2001)
```

### Mesocycle Phase Assessment

```
IF current_week is Week 3 of 4-week block:
  → Volume should be reduced 30–40% from Week 2 peak.
    Intensity maintained at moderate level.
    This is the supercompensation window — adequate nutrition and
    sleep are prerequisites for adaptation (Meeusen et al. 2013).

IF current_week is Week 2 of 4-week block:
  → This is the overloading week — highest combined volume and
    intensity. Monitor ACWR and wellness markers closely.
    Expect mild functional overreaching that will be recovered
    in Week 3.
```
